# Stronger certification, separate from the primary verifier

`information_sets.py` implements a limited exact special case of information-set enumeration. Choose s pairwise-disjoint k-coordinate sets, each of full rank. Row-reduce a generator to identity on each set. Enumerate all row combinations of sizes 1 through t in each such basis. A codeword not enumerated in any basis has at least t+1 ones in each information set, hence weight at least s(t+1). Once this lower bound reaches the smallest encountered weight, that weight is the exact distance. Enumeration to t=k also exhausts the code. Overlapping or dependent sets are rejected.

On the shipped extended BCH controls, compare the certified result to **both** exhaustive checkers. The benchmark counts enumeration visits, which may include duplicates between bases. It does not equate visits saved with a wall-clock speedup. The routine shares rank/systematic utilities with the main implementation and remains experimental; the accepted pilot matrices still use both exhaustive checks.

The full Brouwer–Zimmermann algorithm can exploit partially overlapping information sets and rank deficiencies. Those refinements need additional proved covering bounds before adoption. See the [Magma description](https://docs.magma-maths.org/CodingTheory/LinearCodes/structural.html). SAT with independently checked UNSAT proof traces, exact branch-and-bound with a complete partition certificate, and exact rational LP dual certificates are separate future routes. A found low-weight word only proves an upper bound on a fixed code's distance; a timeout or failed random search proves no lower bound.

`extension_obstruction.py` handles a different exact question. For every minimum-weight message m of a fixed generator, an added column x must satisfy m·x=1 to increase d by one. Gaussian elimination either finds x or an odd collection of minimum messages whose XOR is zero, certifying inconsistency. The obstruction checker validates those minimum words and their dependency. This rules out a one-column improvement of **that code**, not the existence of any better code at the new parameters.
