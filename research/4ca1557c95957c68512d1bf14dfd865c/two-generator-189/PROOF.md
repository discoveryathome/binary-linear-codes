# Necessary high-distance CRT profiles and the exact scope of the null results

All statements and computations here are **author-checked by one AI agent session**.
Independent coding-theorist review remains open. The structural reduction below is
not a full classification of codes or a proof that [189,22,73] is impossible.

## Shift model and factorization

Use three blocks of 63 binary coordinates, with the same one-position cyclic shift
on every block. Such a binary linear code is an R-submodule of R^3 for
`R=GF(2)[x]/(x^63+1)`. The standard module and squarefree CRT description is given in
Güneri, Ling, and Özkaya, [Quasi-Cyclic Codes, Sections 2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2007.16029v2),
following the original [Ling–Solé finite-field treatment](https://doi.org/10.1109/18.959257).
The specialized exclusions and computations below are this package's deductions.

The complete factorization, checked by exact polynomial division, has degrees
`1,2,3,3,6,6,6,6,6,6,6,6,6`. In hexadecimal coefficient encoding the factors are

```
3; 7; b,d; 43,49,57,5b,61,67,6d,73,75.
```

The factors are distinct, since 63 is odd in characteristic two. A constituent
associated with a degree-r factor is a linear subspace of K^3 over K=GF(2^r), of
field dimension e in `{0,1,2,3}`. Its binary dimension contribution is r e.

## Excluding higher constituent multiplicities for distance at least 73

A constituent of field dimension at least two contains a nonzero field line with
one coordinate zero: take the kernel of a coordinate projection. Its inverse CRT
image is a binary dimension-r subcode supported on at most two length-63 blocks.

- For r=3, binary distance 73 at length 126 would violate the Griesmer inequality
  `73+37+19=129>126`. Such a constituent forces distance at most 72.
- For r=6, distance 65 at length 126 would violate
  `65+33+17+9+5+3=132>126`. Such a constituent forces distance at most 64.
- A degree-two constituent of field dimension three contains an axis subcode of
  length 63 and dimension two, with distance at most 42.
- A degree-one constituent of dimension three contains an axis word of weight 63.

Thus a possible distance-73 code must have degree-one and degree-two dimensions
at most two, and each degree-three/six constituent dimension at most one.
The [Griesmer inequality](https://doi.org/10.1147/rd.45.0532) is the same exact bound
used by the repository closure engine; its general proof is not re-proved here.

There cannot be two active degree-three lines either. A nonzero symbol in either
minimal cyclic [63,3] constituent has weight 36. If any line has a zero coordinate,
it has support on at most two blocks and distance at most 72. Otherwise its first
coordinate is nonzero, so the joint rank-six constituent code has an injective first
projection. Both factors have order seven. Reduce to three length-seven blocks,
repeated nine times: the first block is the even cyclic [7,6] code generated by x+1,
and the other blocks are multipliers of it. Every resulting word is even. Griesmer
excludes distance ten at [21,6], since `10+5+3+2+1+1=22>21`; evenness improves the
upper bound from nine to eight. Repetition therefore gives distance at most 72.

`pricing.json` exhausts all 4,096 normalized multiplier pairs in this small model.
The independent checker recovers their exact distances and multiplicities, verifies
evenness, and confirms the attainable maximum eight. This corroborates the argument;
the separate zero-coordinate case above is needed for completeness.

## Three necessary aggregate profiles

Let e1,e2 be the degree-one/two constituent dimensions, t3 the number of active
degree-three lines, and t6 the number of active degree-six lines. After those
exclusions, `e1,e2<=2`, `t3<=1`, and

```
e1 + 2 e2 + 3 t3 + 6 t6 = 22.
```

The only solutions are

| Profile | e1 | e2 | t3 | t6 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A | 1 | 0 | 1 | 3 |
| B | 0 | 2 | 0 | 3 |
| C | 2 | 1 | 0 | 3 |

These are necessary constituent patterns, not sufficient conditions for distance 73.
All active degree-six lines must have three nonzero entries; otherwise the same
binary [126,6] bound forces distance at most 64. Normalize their first entries to
one and write their other entries as f_i,g_i in GF(64)*.

In A the degree-three line also has three nonzero entries, while the degree-one
line must have support at least two. A suitable block projection is therefore
injective at binary dimension 22. This is the inherited one-generator search
representation. B and C require two R-generators, because one constituent has
field dimension two; their individual block projections have ranks 20 and 21.

## Canonical forms B and C

In B, a degree-two field plane containing an axis would give distance 42. Its
parity-check coefficients must all be nonzero. Independent cyclic rotations of
the blocks act by every nonzero GF(4) scalar, and normalize the plane to a+b+c=0.

In C, a binary plane containing an axis would give a word of weight 63. Its only
remaining form is the even-parity plane a+b+c=0 over GF(2). The degree-two field
line must have three nonzero entries. Support one gives distance 42. For support
two, add the degree-one plane word on those two blocks: each nonzero degree-two
symbol has period-three weight two, and complementing its three bits changes this
to weight one. Repeated 21 times, the resulting full word has weight 42. With three
nonzero entries, independent block rotations normalize the field line to (1,1,1),
while preserving the degree-one plane.

Both canonical low constituents are binary [9,4,4] codes repeated 21 times, hence
have distance 84. The complete finite checks cover all 21 GF(4) planes and all
7 binary planes times 21 GF(4) lines. Their ranks, CRT equations, exact spectra,
and support distinctions are checked separately. These normalizations preserve
the possible nonzero degree-six multipliers, so the model does not discard an
eligible distance-73 code within profiles B/C up to the stated block rotations.

Choose any three distinct degree-six factors: 84 choices. For each, adding six
binary rows for the normalized field line gives dimension `4+3*6=22`. The explicit
row formulas are in TASK.md and model.py. This parameterization is not an
unrestricted coordinate-equivalence classification.

Every B/C word is even. B has no degree-one component, so each block is even.
In C the degree-one plane contributes even total block parity. Thus reaching
distance at least 73 requires distance at least 74 in either new profile. Their
favorable low-constituent upper bound 84 leaves room; it is not a lower bound for
the full dimension-22 code.

## What the B/C experiment establishes

The fixed seeded procedure made 199,296 proposals, representing 163,272 distinct
profile/factor/scalar choices, with 36,024 repeated choices. It sampled all 168
profile/triple templates before bounded restarts on the sixteen strongest initial
templates. The seed derivation, PRNG, fitness, mutation and restart rules are in
the pinned sources and metadata. No held-out evaluation or algorithm superiority
claim is made.

For each proposal the ledger retains a nonzero message and its exact word weight.
Early rejections store an upper bound on distance; they are not labelled exact
minima. Every saved weight is at most 70. Both best matrices attain distance 70,
with multiplicities 252 in B and 315 in C, checked by full spectra. Consequently
the exact maximum over the retained tested list is 70. This says nothing about
untested B/C parameters and does not establish a universal bound 70 for the family.
It is below the inherited published distance 72 and does not meet the target.

## Complete finite field-scalar neighborhood of the published parent

After that bounded failure, one pivot fixed the published parent's factors and
degree-one line and normalized its degree-three line to (1,1,1). Rotating blocks
two and three by six and five positions does this. Exact row-space equality and
both spectra confirm a coordinate-equivalent [189,22,72] parent, with 2,394 minimum
words. The degree-three factor is d, the degree-six factors are 61,6d,75 (hex),
and the six normalized scalars are `[15,14,8,34,6,60]` (decimal).

Replace zero, one, or two scalar coordinates by any other nonzero field element.
The exact domain has

```
1 + 6*62 + choose(6,2)*62^2 = 58,033 choices.
```

The complete ledger has every choice exactly once in fixed order. Each has an
explicit nonzero word of weight at most 72. The parent itself attains 72, so the
maximum of this finite neighborhood is exactly 72. A separate direct replay checks
the full domain, every witness, the parent's exact spectrum, and the recorded
coordinate permutation. This is not a bound for three or more scalar changes,
factor changes, other low-degree patterns, or the full profile-A family.
