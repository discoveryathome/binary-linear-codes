# A bound for two 85-coordinate cyclic blocks and two appended coordinates

Evidence level: **author-checked**, one AI agent session. This combines an exact
finite witness calculation with an analytic CRT argument. Separate implementations
and automated case checks are not independent human review.

## Statements and scope

Arrange 170 binary coordinates as two blocks of length 85. Let T simultaneously
rotate each block by one coordinate. For every binary linear [170,21] code C with
`T(C)=C`,

```
d(C) <= 64.
```

The bound is attained by the inherited published core in `run/parent-core-170-21-64.json`.
Consequently, appending any two binary generator columns gives distance at most 66,
and the inherited published [172,21,66] code attains that bound.

More generally, if a binary [172,21] code becomes T-invariant after puncturing two
coordinates, it has distance at most 66. This is not a bound for unrestricted
[170,21] or [172,21] codes. Block length 85, the two-block shift invariance, and
binary linearity are essential assumptions.

The computational calculation covers a normalized family with injective first
projection. Sections below handle a full-rank second projection by swapping blocks,
and the remaining projection cases analytically. We do not claim to enumerate all
QC codes or all appended-column pairs individually.

## Factorization and the normalized family

Work in GF(2)[x], with

```
M = x^85 + 1
P = x^5 + 1 = (x+1)(x^4+x^3+x^2+x+1).
```

M has those degree-one and degree-four irreducible factors and ten distinct
degree-eight irreducible factors:

```
139 13f 177 17b 18b 1a3 1bd 1d7 1dd 1f9  (hexadecimal)
```

Bit i encodes the coefficient of x^i. The replay verifies irreducibility and the
complete product, enumerates all 4,096 divisors, and checks that the monic degree-21
divisors are exactly

```
h = P A B
```

for distinct degree-eight factors A and B. There are 45 pairs. Equivalently, solving
`a + 4 b + 8 c = 21` with `a,b` in `{0,1}` forces `a=b=1,c=2`.

Put `g=M/h`. The normalized code for a multiplier f modulo h is

```
C_f = { (g m, g f m mod M) : deg(m)<21 }.
```

The first block is injective: for nonzero m, the nonzero polynomial g m has degree
at most 84. Every C_f therefore has dimension 21. Adding a multiple of h to f changes
the second block by a multiple of M, so all `2^21` residues are included. The exact
computational domain has `45 * 2^21 = 94,371,840` parameter choices; equivalent codes
can occur more than once.

## Why an injective block projection has this form

Identify each block with R=GF(2)[x]/(M). T-invariance makes C an R-submodule of R^2.
If projection onto the first block is injective, its image U is a cyclic ideal of
binary dimension 21, generated by a divisor g of M with degree 64. Thus h=M/g has
degree 21. The projection is an R-module isomorphism from C to U, so C is the graph
of an R-linear map F:U -> R.

Let v=F(g). Since h g=0 in R, R-linearity gives h v=0 in R. Polynomial divisibility
then yields `M | h v`, hence `g | v`. Write `v=g f`; f is determined modulo h. Every
codeword is therefore `(g m,g f m mod M)`, as enumerated above. If the second block
projection is injective, swap the two coordinate blocks and use the same argument.

## Finite witness bound for every normalized multiplier

First restrict messages to `m=P u`, with `deg(u)<16`, and put H=A B. The resulting
dimension-16 subcode consists of

```
( (M/H)u, (M/H) f u mod M ).
```

It depends only on f modulo H. Changing f by Ht adds M t u to the second block, which
vanishes modulo M. A nonzero u of weight at most 64 rejects all full multipliers
with that residue.

Multiplication of f by x modulo h rotates only the second block, preserving weights.
Modulo H it partitions the 65,536 residues into orbits of length dividing 85. The
replay verifies their disjointness, complete coverage, and canonical least residues.
It handles shorter orbits; in particular, the pair `(139,1d7)` has 3,856 orbits.

For any full f, rotate its residue modulo H to a representative r. The rotated full
multiplier is uniquely `r + Ht`, with `deg(t)<5`. Thus all 32 lifts are covered. Either
a restricted witness rejects r and every lift, or explicit full-code witnesses
reject all 32 lifts. Rotating the second block back does not change the weight.

The retained manifest contains:

- 38,016 multiplier-residue orbits across all 45 divisors.
- 31,536 nonzero restricted rejection witnesses `[f,u,w]`, using full message P u.
- 6,480 unresolved residues, discharged by all 32 lifts.
- 207,360 nonzero full rejection witnesses `[f,m,w]`.
- No threshold survivor. Every saved weight is at most 64.

Every witness is nonzero because its first block is nonzero by the injectivity
argument. The direct replay reconstructs both blocks by polynomial convolution and
folding modulo M, without importing the search implementation or its weight tables.
This proves `d(C_f)<=64` for the complete normalized family.

The search also reduces message space by simultaneous block rotation. That makes
threshold screening cheaper, but the final replay relies only on explicit messages
and complete multiplier coverage, not on the search-side message reduction.

## The remaining block projection cases

The standard QC module and CRT decomposition are described by Güneri, Ling, and
Özkaya in [Quasi-Cyclic Codes, arXiv:2007.16029v2, Sections 2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2007.16029v2#S3).
The original finite-field treatment is Ling and Solé,
[On the algebraic structure of quasi-cyclic codes I](https://doi.org/10.1109/18.959257).
The dimension and axis argument below is this package's specialization to block
length 85 and binary dimension 21.

Since the factors of M are distinct, R is the product of the corresponding fields.
The CRT idempotents split an R-submodule C of R^2 into constituents C_i contained in
K_i^2. Each constituent has K_i-dimension e_i in `{0,1,2}`, contributing
`degree(K_i/GF(2))*e_i` to the binary dimension. Hence

```
e1 + 4 e4 + 8 sum(e8) = 21.
```

With `e1,e4` in `{0,1,2}`, the unique aggregate solution is
`e1=e4=1` and `sum(e8)=2`. Thus either one degree-eight constituent has dimension
two, or two distinct degree-eight constituents are lines. The degree-one and
degree-four constituents are always lines.

If a degree-eight constituent has dimension two, it equals K_i^2 and contains an
axis K_i x {0}. Setting every other constituent to zero gives a binary dimension-eight
subcode supported on a single 85-coordinate block. Its distance is at most 40 by
the binary Griesmer bound: distance 41 would require length

```
41 + 21 + 11 + 6 + 3 + 2 + 1 + 1 = 86 > 85.
```

Otherwise the four active constituents are lines, of binary contribution 1,4,8,8.
A non-axis line projects injectively onto each coordinate; an axis line loses its
dimension in exactly one projection. If neither block projection has rank 21,
there must be an axis of each orientation. Since there is only one degree-one
constituent, at least one such axis has degree four or eight. Its inverse CRT image
is a subcode supported on one block, of binary dimension four or eight.

The degree-eight case again has distance at most 40. The dimension-four case has
distance at most 44: distance 45 would require

```
45 + 23 + 12 + 6 = 86 > 85.
```

These are direct applications of the binary
[Griesmer bound](https://doi.org/10.1147/rd.45.0532), also used by the repository's
closure engine. Consequently every remaining projection case has distance at most
44, which is already below 64. Automated structural checks confirm the dimension
pattern, both integer sums, and all 81 axis/non-axis line projection patterns; they
do not replace review of the CRT argument.

Combining the injective-projection calculation and these remaining cases proves
the stated bound for every T-invariant binary [170,21] code.

## Two appended coordinates and attainment

For any nonzero core word of weight at most 64, two additional binary coordinates
add at most two to its weight. Every two-column extension therefore has distance
at most 66, with no period-five or constant-tail restriction.

If puncturing two coordinates from a binary [172,21] code loses dimension, a nonzero
word lies in the puncturing kernel and is supported entirely on those two positions;
the original distance is then at most two. Otherwise the punctured core has dimension
21 and the bound above applies whenever it is T-invariant.

The inherited core attains 64, with 2,040 minimum words, and its published two-column
extension attains 66, with 2,720 minimum words. The search-side Gray and separate
subset-halves checkers agree on the core. The direct replay additionally checks both
full 2^21-word spectra, the rank, exact core-to-parent puncturing, and polynomial
coordinates. Thus the bounds are attained within the stated families.
